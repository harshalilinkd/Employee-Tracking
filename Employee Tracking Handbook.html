What this is

A record of what people actually did — recognition when someone does well, a goofup when something goes wrong — so a review conversation rests on dated entries rather than on whoever remembers loudest.

Everything on every screen is built from those entries. Nothing is estimated, inferred, or filled in. If nobody recorded it, the system does not know it happened and will not pretend otherwise.

The one habit that matters

The system is only as good as what gets entered. Two minutes at the time beats an hour of recall at appraisal. An employee with no entries shows No signal — not a good score.

Your access

Management and Executive Assistants both record performance, but they are not the same account. An Executive Assistant records and fixes their own entries; changing the system itself, correcting someone else's entry, and reading the audit log stay with Super Admin and Management.

Enforced in the database

A Department Manager does not merely have rows hidden from their screen — the database refuses to return them. Nobody can reach another department's data by any route, including by calling the API directly.

The screens

Dashboard everyone

What needs attention right now, for the date range you pick at the top right.

  • Four cards — headcount, goofups, who needs attention, who was recognised.
  • Management Attention — only people who need action. If it is empty, that is genuinely good news.
  • Quick Insights — each line pairs a number with what to do about it, and links to the filtered list.
  • Impact Mix — how serious the recorded goofups are. Recognitions are not counted here; they carry no impact grade.
  • Issue heat — weighted goofup load per person per month. Darker is heavier.

Lists say how much they are hiding. Showing 6 of 23 with a Show all button — never a silent top-six.

Performance everyone

The full ledger — every entry, searchable and filterable. This is where you go to check a specific claim.

  • Filter by employee, department, type, category, impact or period. The impact filter disappears when you filter to positive contributions, because those carry no impact.
  • Saved views keep a filter combination you use often.
  • Export CSV gives you exactly the rows currently filtered.
  • Each row has a ⋮ menu — view, edit, archive, delete.

Reports everyone

One employee, one period, formatted to print and sign. Covered in detail below.

Settings Super Admin & MD

  • Employee database — add, edit, deactivate. Edit rows lets you change several people at once without opening a form. Update emails takes a pasted list rather than fifty forms.
  • Performance categories — what people pick from when recording. Goofup categories carry a default impact; positive ones do not.
  • Observers — the names offered under Who observed this? Kept separate from the employee database, because the people who witness events are usually not the people being assessed.
  • User access — who can sign in, and as what.
  • Audit log — every change, grouped by the record it happened to.
  • General — the numbers behind the signal. Editable, but read the warning there first.

Employee profile everyone

Click any name. Their full timeline, recognitions and goofups separately, and how their signal was reached.

Recording an event

Press Record Performance — top right on desktop, the middle button in the bottom bar on a phone.

  1. Pick the person

    Type to search. Only active employees appear.

  2. Positive contribution, or goofup

    This decides which categories you are offered next — and whether you are asked for an impact at all.

  3. Choose a category

    Quality Issue, Delay, Teamwork, Leadership and so on. A goofup category carries a default impact, which you can override.

  4. Set the impact — goofups only

    Low, Medium, High or Critical. This is the single most consequential field on the form; the weights are below. You will not see it on a positive contribution — the scale grades how bad something was, which says nothing about a recognition.

  5. Say who observed it

    Optional, and drawn from the Observers list in Settings. This is the person who saw it, which is often not the person typing it in — who is recorded separately and cannot be changed.

  6. Write what happened, and save

    One specific sentence. "Done late — 4 days" is useful in six months; "poor performance" is not.

Two rules you will meet

You cannot record against yourself. Not even as Super Admin. The database refuses it.

You have 48 hours to fix your own entry. After that only Management can change it — so history cannot be quietly rewritten weeks later. Every change is logged either way.

Fixing an entry

Every row in the ledger carries a ⋮ menu on the right. It offers four things, and greys out the ones you cannot use with the reason attached — so a missing option is never a mystery.

ActionWhat happensWho
View The full record — description, observer, recorder, attachments, and its own change history. Anyone who can see the record
Edit Title, description, date, category and impact. Employee, type and who recorded it are frozen at creation. Management any time; the recorder for 48 hours
Archive Asks for a reason. The record leaves the ledger and stops counting toward signals, but is kept and stays readable in the audit trail. Management any time; the recorder for 48 hours
Delete Gone permanently, with its attachments. No confirmation step — it goes immediately. Super Admin and MD only
Archive, unless it was never real

Archive is the one you almost always want. It hides the record and keeps the history, so if the entry is ever questioned there is still something to produce.

Delete cannot be undone and fires on the first click. The deletion itself is written to the audit log with your name — so there is a record that a record was removed — but the entry itself is gone. Use it for something entered in error, not for something inconvenient.

The signal

Each employee gets one word: Strong Stable Watch Attention No signal. It is not recognitions minus goofups. Three things shape it.

Impact is weighted

One critical goofup outweighs six low ones. These are the current weights:

ImpactWeightMeaning
Low1Minor, easily corrected
Medium2Noticeable, some rework
High3.5Real cost to the business
Critical6Serious — customer, safety or compliance
Recognitions all count the same

The weights above grade goofups. A positive contribution carries no impact, so every recognition counts once — nobody can lift someone's band by grading a compliment "critical".

Old events fade

An event carries roughly half its weight after about a month (a 45-day decay). Something from last week matters more than the same thing from two months ago. Nothing older than 90 days counts at all.

Silence is not a pass

Fewer than 3 events and the system says No signal — never Stable. Someone nobody has observed is a different problem from someone doing fine, and the system refuses to confuse the two.

Reading the bands

The rules are checked in this order. The first one that matches wins.

#BandTriggered when
1 Attention Any critical goofup at all — checked before everything else, including the 3-event minimum
2 No signal Fewer than 3 events in the window
3 Attention Weighted issue load reaches 7, or 2+ overdue follow-ups
4 Watch Issue load reaches 4, or the same category repeats, or the trend drops sharply
5 Strong Recognition load reaches 6, issues stay under 3, and the trend is not falling
6 Stable Issue load under 4 and nothing overdue
Why rule 1 sits above rule 2

A single critical goofup flags Attention immediately, even for someone with only that one entry. Waiting for a third event before surfacing a safety or compliance failure would defeat the point of recording it.

Every badge explains itself. Hover it, or open the profile, and you get the sentence behind the verdict — how many events, what load, what trend. If it cannot be explained, it does not ship.

Adding people

A new employee to track

Settings → Employee database → Add employee. Name, department, designation, who they report to, status, and an optional email. They appear immediately in the record form.

Someone who needs to sign in

Settings → User access → Give someone access. Enter their Gmail address and pick a role.

Invite only — this is the important one

Signing in with Google does not grant access. Only people whose email you have added here can get in. Everyone else is refused, however valid their Google account.

Their row shows Pending until they sign in for the first time, then Linked. Nothing is wrong while it says Pending.

Someone who witnesses events

Settings → Observers → Add observer. A name, and optionally a note like MD or Floor supervisor — Weaving. Observers do not need a login and do not need to be employees.

To retire one, deactivate rather than delete: it leaves the dropdown while every past record still shows who observed it. The system blocks deleting an observer that any record names.

Removing people

Two different actions. Pick the right one.

ActionWhat happensUse when
Deactivate Removed from the record form and the dashboard. All history kept and readable. Reversible. Almost always — someone left, or a login is no longer needed
Delete Gone permanently. Cannot be undone. Only a row added by mistake
The system will stop you

An employee with any recorded event cannot be deleted. Their history would be left with nobody attached to it, so the database refuses and offers Deactivate instead.

A user who has recorded events cannot be deleted either, for the same reason — their entries would lose their author. And you cannot delete your own login, so you cannot lock yourself out.

The MD report

Reports → pick the employee → set From and To → Download PDF.

One page: letterhead, the person, their signal, key figures, activity by month, every event in the period, and a signature block for Prepared / Reviewed / Approved. Print and CSV sit beside it.

Set this once in the print dialog

Tick Background graphics, or the tinted panels print white. Untick Headers and footers to drop the browser's date and URL. Chrome remembers both.

The employee dropdown lists only people who have at least one recorded event. A report on someone with no entries would be a blank page.

The audit trail

Every change is written by the database itself, not by the app — so it is recorded even if a change is made outside this system. One save is one entry, however many fields it touched.

One record at a time

Open any event and scroll to History: created, edited, archived, restored, each with who, when, and every field that changed — old value to new. This is the fastest answer to "why does this entry say that?"

Everything at once

Settings → Audit log lists records, not individual changes. Open one to see its whole history in the same form. Search by record, by person, or by type. Deleted records stay listed, marked no longer exists — that is the point of an audit trail.

What is not logged

Housekeeping columns the system maintains itself, and saves that changed nothing. If you open a record, change nothing and save, no entry is written.

What it won't do

These are deliberate. Knowing them saves you hunting for a setting that does not exist.

  • It will not guess. No estimated scores, no filled gaps. Fewer than three events shows No signal.
  • It will not let you score yourself. No role can.
  • It will not let history be quietly edited. 48 hours for your own entry, Management beyond that, and every change logged. Management can delete an entry outright — and that deletion is logged too.
  • It will not orphan a record. Anyone with history cannot be deleted, only deactivated.
  • It will not grade a recognition. Impact applies to goofups; a positive contribution has none to give.
  • It will not rank people against each other. The signal describes one person against their own record, not a league table.
If something looks wrong

Check the date range first — it is the most common cause. The dashboard defaults to the last 90 days, and an event outside that window will not appear anywhere on the page.
